Methodology

Rigour you can defend
in front of an investment committee.

Most tree tools hand-wave the statistics. Arbor's engine enforces the rules that make a stochastic model defensible — they are architecture, not options.

§ 01
Distributions live on the right things
Uncertainty belongs to chance-node probabilities and terminal payoffs. A decision is a choice, not a random variable — Arbor will never let you put a distribution on one.
§ 02
Stochastic stays stochastic
Once a value is described by a distribution, it is sampled fresh in every iteration. It is never quietly collapsed back to a point estimate to make the maths easier.
§ 03
Normalisation happens inside the iteration
Sampled sibling probabilities are renormalised within each iteration's rollback — never pre-normalised on the model, which would confuse sampling variance with parameter belief.
§ 04
The tree is rolled back every iteration
Mean of rollbacks ≠ rollback of means — Jensen's inequality guarantees it. Arbor computes EV inside each iteration, then aggregates, and reports how often the optimal path itself changes.
Workflow

From assumption to
defensible answer.

01
Sketch the decision
Decision, chance, and terminal nodes on an infinite canvas. Drag, branch, and label — the structure of the choice comes first, before any numbers.
02
Describe the uncertainty
Every probability and payoff accepts a point estimate or a full distribution — triangular, beta, uniform, lognormal, truncated normal. Live preview as you type.
03
Run ten thousand futures
A seeded Monte Carlo samples every assumption, rolls the tree back inside each iteration, and streams convergence live onto the canvas. Same seed, same answer — byte for byte.
04
Read the verdict — then stress it
EV distribution with percentiles, optimal-path stability, tornado sensitivity on every stochastic driver. Export the full audit trail to Excel with live formulas.
